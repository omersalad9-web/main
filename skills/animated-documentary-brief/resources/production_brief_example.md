## 6. VOICEOVER PRODUCTION (DAY 3–4)

**Generate audio before any visuals.**

### ElevenLabs settings
- **Model:** v3 (or latest)
- **Voice:** Test *Brian*, *Adam*, *Bill* — choose gravity over warmth; British accent reads as authoritative for historical documentary
- **Stability:** 75–80
- **Similarity:** 80
- **Style exaggeration:** 0 (flatness is the point)
- **Speaker boost:** ON

### Workflow
1. Paste script in ~500-word chunks (one act at a time)
2. Generate each act separately
3. Listen critically — any line that sounds dramatic: regenerate
4. Export each act as WAV at 48kHz
5. Lay out on DaVinci timeline first — this is the spine

### Pacing notes
Use bracketed directions in ElevenLabs:
- `[pause]` — adds a beat
- `[long pause]` — adds 2+ seconds
- `<break time="2s" />` — SSML (works on some plans)

## 7. EDIT PRINCIPLES

### Fern rhythm rules
1. Cut on the breath, not the beat
2. Hold shots 9–12 seconds (longer than feels comfortable)
3. At least 3 moments of complete silence mapped in advance:
   - Cold open title card (3 sec)
   - End of darkest act (4 sec)
   - Death/consequences moment in resolution (3 sec)
4. Mirror cold open visuals exactly at the close

### Music map (Unit 731)

### Colour grade (DaVinci)
- **Historical LUT:** Desaturated, teal-shadow shift, lifted blacks, film grain overlay
- **Modern LUT:** Warm amber highlights, orange-teal contrast, sharper

### Captions
- Font: clean sans-serif, white with thin black stroke
- Position: lower third
- Timing: appear with narration, not before
- Manually clean Japanese/unusual names after auto-caption

## THE ONE THING THAT DETERMINES WHETHER THIS WORKS

Lock the voiceover first. Build everything to its timing.

The Fern look isn't the clips — it's the *patience* of the edit. A 9-second shot of an empty corridor with the right narration over it is worth ten busy AI-generated action shots.
