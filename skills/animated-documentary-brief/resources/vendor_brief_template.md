## 3. AUDIO REQUIREMENTS

### Narration

- The full script must be narrated end-to-end
- Voice character: male, mid-range to deep, slow, measured, dry, documentary register. **Authoritative, not theatrical.** The delivery should feel restrained even on the heaviest material — flatness is more powerful than drama on this subject
- Accent flexibility: British or neutral mid-Atlantic preferred. Avoid heavily American or heavily regional voices
- Pacing: approximately 145–155 words per minute. Honour the pauses and breath beats marked in the script
- Consistent across the full runtime — no audible cuts or tonal shifts between sections
- Delivered at 48kHz minimum, broadcast quality, no compression artefacts

### Music

- Original or properly licensed
- Sparse and atmospheric. The video should have multiple stretches of intentional near-silence (especially in the central acts dealing with the experiments)
- Score must escalate and de-escalate with the script's emotional arc, not run continuously underneath
- No royalty issues. All licensing must be transferable to the client for YouTube monetisation and any future re-uploads

### Sound design

- Layered, subtle, naturalistic. Footsteps, distant wind, room tone, equipment hum where appropriate
- No theatrical stings or jump-scare effects. This is documentary, not horror
- All ambient beds licensed cleanly

## 4. VISUAL REQUIREMENTS

### Overall standard

Every shot must look intentional, cinematic, and period-appropriate. The visual language should suggest a feature documentary, not an animated explainer.

The video covers three distinct visual periods. Each requires a different treatment:

- **Modern present-day (cold open and closing mirror)** — photorealistic, warm, contemporary documentary aesthetic. Quiet, beautiful, almost serene
- **Pre-war and wartime era (Acts 1–6)** — desaturated, cold, period-accurate. The visual mood is institutional, industrial, austere
- **Contemporary memorial / aftermath (final act)** — sombre present-day footage of memorial spaces, museum exhibits, restrained and reverent

A consistent grading approach must run through each period. The visual contrast between *warm modern* and *cold historical* is part of the video's thesis. This must be visible to the viewer without being announced.

### Shot inventory

The script will be broken into approximately 85–95 individual shots across 8 sections. A detailed shot list will be provided once the script is parsed (you may also produce the shot list yourself as part of pre-production — agree the approach upfront).

Each shot must be:

- Period-accurate where historical
- Free of identifiable real people unless using genuine archival material of deceased public figures where public-domain photographs exist
- Free of any graphic or explicit content — this video depicts atrocity through **atmosphere, implication, and aftermath**, never through direct visualisation. Empty rooms, closed doors, abandoned objects, landscapes, surgical instruments at rest — never the act itself
- Free of any identifiable copyrighted material

### Archival material

Real archival footage may be used **where it adds credibility and where licensing is clean**. Specifically:

- Public-domain footage from official national archives
- Public-domain photographs of named historical figures
- Properly licensed stock footage where it integrates cleanly with the animated material

The mix between animated and archival should feel seamless. The viewer should not be jolted between them.

### Visual consistency

Across the full runtime, the following must remain visually consistent:

- Aspect ratio: 16:9, 4K (3840×2160) minimum
- Frame rate: 24fps preferred (cinematic), or 30fps if the technical pipeline demands it
- Colour grading: two locked looks — one for historical, one for modern. Applied consistently within each section
- Recurring visual motifs must look like the same locations across multiple shots, not regenerated from scratch each time
- No visible AI artefacts, hallucinated text, broken hands, melting features, or other tell-tale generative errors. If a shot has artefacts, regenerate it

### What the visuals must not do

- No depiction of the events being documented
- No graphic injury, blood, or violence
- No identifiable real victims
- No use of real victim photographs even where they exist in archives — the dignity of victims is paramount
- No "AI fever dream" stylisation. The look is grounded, photographic, restrained
- No on-screen text overlays during narration unless specified (dates, place names, the closing stats card)

## 5. EDIT REQUIREMENTS

The voiceover is the spine of the video. Build everything to its timing.

### Pacing principles

- Cut on natural breath pauses in the narration, not on music beats
- Hold shots longer than feels instinctive. A shot that lives on screen for 8–10 seconds is correct for this format
- At least three moments in the final cut must contain **deliberate silence** — no music, no sfx, just narration and held image. These will be marked in the shot list
- The cold open and closing must visually rhyme. The same opening locations and small human moments appear at both ends of the video — this is the closing argument

### Structural requirements

- Title card at the start, after the cold open hook
- Section breaks marked by brief visual breathing room (a held shot + tonal music shift), never by hard chapter cards
- Closing stats card at the end (data points will be provided)
- End frame held for at least 4 seconds before any subscribe prompts or end screens

### Captions

- Full burnt-in captions, accurate to the narration, professionally timed
- Also delivered as a separate `.srt` file for YouTube upload
- Clean sans-serif typeface, white with subtle stroke, lower third positioning
- All proper nouns checked manually — do not trust auto-generated captions for unusual names, places, or historical terms
- A sensitive-content warning card before the cold open

Alongside the master video, you must deliver:

### Thumbnail

Three thumbnail concepts, each at 1920×1080, designed for high click-through. The thumbnail style should match top-performing historical documentary channels — single strong image, bold short text, high contrast, cinematic colour palette. The client will select one for publication.

### Title options

Three YouTube title options. Each under 60 characters. Each must convey the cover-up angle, which is the strongest hook of this story.

### Description

A YouTube description of approximately 200–400 words including:

- A 2–3 sentence hook
- Chapter timestamps based on the final cut

### Metadata

- Suggested tags
- Suggested category
- Suggested upload time window for first-48-hour algorithm performance

## 9. CREATIVE GUARDRAILS (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

Independent of technical choices, the following are firm:

1. **The script is not to be altered.** No line removals, no rephrasing, no additions, no shifts in word order. If a line is genuinely impossible to narrate or visualise, flag it for the client's decision
2. **No graphic content.** Atmosphere and implication only. If you are unsure whether a shot is appropriate, default to restraint
3. **Victim dignity is paramount.** No real victim photographs. No re-enactments of suffering. No close-ups of distressed faces, real or generated
4. **No identifiable real people** other than named historical perpetrators where public-domain photographs exist
5. **No fabricated quotes or statistics.** The script's figures have been researched. Do not add new claims. Do not invent dialogue
6. **Tonal restraint throughout.** No horror conventions. No dramatic music stings. No sensational framing. This is documentary, not entertainment
7. **All assets must be fully licensed.** Any rights uncertainty disqualifies the asset. The client must be able to monetise and re-broadcast indefinitely without exposure
